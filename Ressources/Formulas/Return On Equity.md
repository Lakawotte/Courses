# Definition
## I. Statement

>[!hint] Definition 1 : Ratio
$$
ROE=\frac{I}{SE}
$$
Where $I$ is the *income* and $SE$ is the shareholder's **[[Equity]]**.

>[!tip] Definition 2 : DuPond Formula
$$
ROE=NPM\times AT\times EM
$$
Where $NPM$ is the *net profit margin*, $AT$ is the *assets turnover* and $EM$ the **[[Equity]]** multiplier.
$$
NPM=\frac{I}{R}
$$
Where $I$ is the *income* and $R$ is the *revenue*.
$$
AT=\frac{R}{A}
$$
Where $A$ is the *total assets*.
$$
EM=\frac{A}{SE}
$$
#### Note :
The **net profit margin** can be seen as a measurement of profitability as a ratio of revenue.
The **assets turnover** indicates efficiency in using assets to generate sales.
The **equity multiplier** reflects financial leverage (the extent of using debt to finance assets).

# Application
## I. Meaning
The **return on equity** is a measurement on how efficiently a company uses its shareholder's **[[Equity]]** to generate profits.
It can help indicates the profitability and financial health of the company.

**ROE** can be used to determine the profitability of a company relative to the equity invested by shareholders. We can compare **ROEs** across companies in the same industry to identify well-managed and high-performing ones.
A high **ROE** also indicates that the company is using important leverage, which in this case leads to a risk increase. Respectively, a low **ROE** could mean that a company operates conservatively, with less risk.
