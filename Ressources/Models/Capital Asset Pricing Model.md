# Definition
## I. Statement

>[!hint] Definition
$$
\mathbb{E}(R_{i})=R_{f}+\beta_{i}(\mathbb{E}(R_{m})-R_{f})
$$
Where $\mathbb{E}(R_i)$ and $\mathbb{E}(R_m)$ are the **[[Expected Value|expected values]]** of the **[[Return|returns]]** on the financial asset $i$  and the market's return, $R_f$ is the **[[risk-free rate of return]]** and $\beta_i$ the **[[β]]** value of the financial asset $i$.
#### Note :
More precisely, $\mathbb{E}(R_m)-R_f$ is the **[[market premium]]** and $\mathbb{E}(R_i)-R_f$ is the **[[individual risk premium]]**.
#### Note :
In most cases we use the **[[Mean|arithmetic mean]]** of the market's **[[Return]]** to estimate $\mathbb{E}(R_{m})$ and we do so with the **[[risk-free rate of return]]** instead of choosing the current $R_f$.

# Application
## I. Meaning
The **CAPM** is a widely used model that establishes a linear relationship between the **required return** and **risk**.
This model has some drawnbacks due to its underlying assumptions that many considers unrealistic.
In most situations, its preferable to use the **CAPM** instead of others models like the **[[Maximum Drawdown]]** or the **[[Weighted Average Cost of Capital]]**.
