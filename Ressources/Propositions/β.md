# Definition
## I. Statement

>[!hint] Definition 1 : **[[Return]]**
>$$
\beta=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(R_{e},R_{m})}{\mathbb{V}\mathrm{ar}(R_{m})}
>$$
>Where $R_e$ is the **[[Return|return on an individual stock]]** and $R_m$ is the **[[Return|return of the overall market]]**.

>[!tip] Definition 2 : **[[Correlation]]**
>$$
\beta=\rho_{i,m}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{m}}
>$$
>Where $\rho_{i,m}$ is the **[[Pearson's Product Moment Correlation Coefficient|Pearson's rho]]** and $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma_m$ are the respective **[[Standard Deviation|standard deviation]]** of the investment and the market.

# Application
## I. Meaning
The **β** of a market tells us about it's **[[Correlation]]** and **[[Volatility]]** compared to a reference market such as the S&P 500. It's mainly used with the **[[Capital Asset Pricing Model]]**.
The **β** of a market can take different values.
	$0\le \beta\le 1$ : the market's **[[Volatility]]** is lesser than the S&P 500's, and its **[[Correlation]]** is low.
	$\beta\ge 1$ : the market's **[[Volatility]]** is greater than the S&P 500's, and its **[[Correlation]]** is high.
	$\beta\le 0$ : the market's **[[Volatility]]** is greater than the S&P 500's, and its **[[Correlation]]** is inversed.
