ROC=\frac{NOPAT}{AIC}
>$$
Where the $NOPAT$ is the *Net Operating Profit After Tax* and the $AIC$ is the *Average Invested Capital*.

# Application
## I. Meaning
# 1. Meaning
The **return on capital** is a fraction of the original investment redistribued with the dividends.

The **ROC** is no considered as a *income* because it's a *refund*. It decreases the *tax base* of the investment, indeed we deduce a certain amount of money from the original investment.

This allows the company to pay the dividends even if they don't have enought profits. In this case, the company still looks stable and profitable even if it's not the case.
Note that it's commonly used in *REITs* and *MLPs*.
## II. Use
This is not a good long term strategy for an investor, due to the fact that it increases the potential taxable gain when the asset is sold. Furthermore, it can reduce the overall value of the action.
