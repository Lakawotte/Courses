# 1. Theory
The **volatility** of a market represents the differences that prices can have regarding the average in a certain amount of time. This provides information regarding the security of the market : a market with a volatility over 1% is considered as a risky and volatile market. Volatility can be compared to the annual [[Standard Deviation]].

# 2. Interpretation
### I
$$
v=\sigma \sqrt{ T }
$$
Where $T$ is the number of periods in the time interval.
### II
The [[β]] of a market indicates it's **volatility** compared to the volatility of another market (mostly the **S&P 500**).

### III
We can check the **CBOE Volatility Index** to get an idea of the future volatility of derived markets. It's a gauge of future bets that investors and traders are making on the direction of the markets or individual securities.
A high reading on the **VIX** implies a risky market.
https://www.boursorama.com/indices/cours/analyses/1cVIX/
#### Note :
The VIX may be manipulated to encourage speculation.
### IV
There is 2 ways of considering volatility :
	The **Implied volatility** is a supposition of how the market's volatility will be in the future. It's one of the most important tools for traders.
	The **Historical volatility** measures the volatility that a market has during a certain period. It's usefull to determine the stability of the market, but it can't be applied to forward-looking strategies.
