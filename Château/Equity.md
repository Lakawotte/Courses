# 1. Theory
**Equity** represents the amount of money that would be returned to a company's shareholders if all of the assets were liquidated and all of the company's debt was paid off in the case of liquidation.
In the case of acquisition, it's the value of company sales minus any liabilities owed by the company not transferred with the sale.
# 2. Interpretation
### I
When the owners of a company are **shareholders**, we can calculate the **shareholder's equity** :
$$
E=A-L
$$
Where $A$ is the **assets** and $L$ the **liabilities**.
Shareholder's equity reflects the owner's claim on the business or the assets. Moreover, it can represent the book value of the company. It can be seen as a degree of residual ownership in a firm or asset after subtracting all debts associated with it.
**Equity** can either be :
	 E>0 : this means that the company have enough capital to invest in projects and is more likely to be stable
	 E<0 : in this case, the company have more debt than incomes, so we can expect it to be unstable
